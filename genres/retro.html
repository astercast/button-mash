Genre deep dive · 15 games

Retro games: insert coin, travel back.

Retro is the cabinet's memory — 44 of the catalog's 213 games. This dive curates fifteen of them: the great archive doors, the DOS canon, the golden-age cabinets, the empire builders, and the platformers and adventures a decade grew up on. Every game below is free in the browser.

» Start here: the archive doors

🕹️

Internet Archive: Internet Arcade

Why it fits: the golden age, whole. Hundreds of arcade classics, preserved and playable — no tokens, no cabinet required. If you bookmark one door into the arcade's best years, make it this one. Insert coin.

Controls: Keyboard (on-screen pad)

📺

Internet Archive: Console Living Room

Why it fits: the console half of the archive: hundreds of vintage console games, legally emulated right in your browser. The living-room machines, without the living room — or the cartridge blowing.

Controls: Keyboard (on-screen pad)

💾

Internet Archive: MS-DOS Library

Why it fits: thousands of DOS games in one library. The record names the shelf's three ambassadors itself — Oregon Trail, DOOM, Prince of Persia — and all three get their own cards below. The other thousands are one search away.

Controls: Keyboard

» The DOS canon

😈

DOOM (1993)

Why it fits: the 1993 shareware episode in the Archive's DOSBox — the game that made the FPS a phenomenon, exactly as it was. Arrow keys to move, Ctrl to fire; some designs never needed improving.

Controls: Arrow keys — move; Ctrl — fire

🐂

The Oregon Trail

Why it fits: the 1990 DOS classic: ford the rivers, hunt the buffalo, and try not to die of dysentery on the trail west. Every choice is supplies against distance, and the trail keeps the receipts.

Controls: Keyboard — menus and hunting

🕯️

Zork I

Why it fits: west of house. It is pitch black. The 1980 text adventure that defined a genre — no graphics, all imagination, right in your browser. Type your commands, read everything twice, and mind the darkness.

Controls: Keyboard (type commands)

» Golden-age arcade

👾

Space Invaders

Why it fits: Taito's 1978 quarter-muncher: four notes of bass, one descending armada, and the birth of the high score. Nearly everything else on this page descends from this one row of aliens.

Controls: Arrow keys / buttons

🚀

Missile Command

Why it fits: Atari's 1980 Cold War nightmare: defend six cities with three batteries, trackball-true. You do not win this one — the record's own verdict stands: the end is not a matter of if.

Controls: Mouse (trackball)

🤖

Robotron: 2084

Why it fits: Williams' 1982 twin-stick masterpiece — the game that popularized the twin-stick shooter. One stick to move, one to fire, and the last human family to rescue from the swarm.

Controls: WASD + arrows (twin-stick)

» Empires and city halls

🏙️

SimCity (1989)

Why it fits: zone land, lay roads, and keep the Sims happy in the city-builder that started it all — Will Wright's 1989 original, playable in the Archive's DOSBox. Every city builder since is arguing with this one.

Controls: Mouse

🏛️

Sid Meier's Civilization

Why it fits: 4000 BC to the space race: found cities, research pottery, and outlast history itself in the 1991 original 4X. The benchmark every later empire game is quietly measured against.

Controls: Mouse + keyboard

🚂

Railroad Tycoon

Why it fits: Sid Meier's 1990 railroad empire: lay track, haul freight, and bankrupt the robber barons. The tycoon formula, written before tycoon was a shelf of its own.

Controls: Mouse

» Jump, point, click

🛸

Commander Keen 3: Keen Must Die!

Why it fits: Billy Blaze's 1990 pogo-stick platforming finale to the Invasion of the Vorticons trilogy — id's shareware-era charmer. Arrow keys, Ctrl, Space, and a pogo stick doing most of the work.

Controls: Arrow keys + Ctrl/Space

⚔️

Prince of Persia

Why it fits: Jordan Mechner's rotoscoped classic in its 1990 DOS port — the Apple II original is 1989. Sixty minutes to save the princess, one leap of faith at a time, and the clock does not blink.

Controls: Arrow keys — run, jump, fight

🏴‍☠️

The Secret of Monkey Island

Why it fits: Guybrush Threepwood wants to be a pirate. Insult-sword-fight your way through Lucasfilm Games' 1990 point-and-click classic — the wittiest card on this page, and it is not close.

Controls: Mouse

What makes a game retro? It was built for a machine you can no longer buy, and it still holds up. These fifteen each preserve a different room of the house — the arcade floor (Space Invaders, Missile Command, Robotron), the DOS desk (DOOM, Zork I, The Oregon Trail), the mayor's office and the map table (SimCity, Civilization, Railroad Tycoon), and the shareware disk (Commander Keen, Prince of Persia, Monkey Island). The other 29 retro games in the catalog — more golden-age cabinets, more DOS classics, the PICO-8 shelf, ClassicReload's DOS shelf, and two browser-native worlds — are all on the directory shelf.
