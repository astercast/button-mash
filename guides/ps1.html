PlayStation emulation guide

The disc era in your browser: a fast, friendly core, one honest BIOS conversation, and a library of RPGs and platformers that defined 3D gaming.

  • Core: psx · PCSX-ReARMed
  • BIOS: boots via HLE; your dumped BIOS improves accuracy
  • ⏱ ~15 min
  • Intermediate

1. The core: PCSX-ReARMed

The Player's psx core is PCSX-ReARMed, the default PlayStation backend in the official EmulatorJS core list (the alternate is Mednafen's PSX HW core). It's the pragmatic pick: fast enough for browsers, accurate enough for the vast majority of the library.

The Player detects .cue, .bin, .iso, .img, .chd, and .m3u files and boots PCSX-ReARMed automatically. For multi-disc games, an .m3u playlist pointing at your own rips keeps disc-swapping simple.

2. BIOS notes: honest, not scary

PCSX-ReARMed boots most games without any BIOS, the core includes a high-level reimplementation of the PlayStation's firmware (HLE). So you can try the system today with zero extra files.

That said, HLE isn't the real thing: a handful of games glitch or refuse to boot without the genuine firmware. The proper fix is dumping the BIOS from your own PlayStation hardware (a scph5501.bin-style dump) and attaching it with the Player's BIOS picker, the Player already warns you about this when you select PlayStation. Never download a BIOS file; they're copyrighted software, exactly like ROMs.

3. Settings that matter

Open the engine's in-game menu (the ⚙ icon) while a PlayStation game is running:

  • Enhanced resolution: render above native PS1 resolution for crisp 3D. 2× is the sweet spot; higher costs real performance.
  • CD fast-load / fast boot: skips the classic boot animation and speeds up disc reads, big quality-of-life win.
  • Dithering: the PS1 dithered everything to hide its color limits; on modern screens it can look noisy. Your call, purists leave it on.
  • Analog toggle: many games support the DualShock analog sticks, the Player's gamepad support and remap tools cover this.

4. Performance: fine on most machines

PS1 in a browser is much lighter than its reputation suggests, any recent laptop or desktop runs it full-speed, and recent phones do fine at native resolution. If it stutters: lower the enhanced resolution back to 1×, close other tabs, and make sure the browser isn't in a throttled low-power state. Keep your own rips in .chd format, much smaller files, same game.

5. Saves & cheats in the Player

  • Save states, made in the engine's in-game menu. With Keep in Browser on (Settings → Save States), the Player 2.0 Save tool on player.html can export/import/delete them.
  • Memory cards, the engine emulates PS1 memory cards, so in-game saving works normally.
  • Cheats, the Player 2.0 Cheats tool takes GameShark codes, stored per game and injected before boot; toggle in the engine's cheat manager.
  • Keyboard remap, the Player 2.0 Controls tool remaps Player 1's keyboard and gamepad bindings (applies on next boot).

6. Play it in the Player

» Open the Player (PlayStation pre-selected)

The ?core=psx deep link just pre-selects the system, your game file still comes from your own machine, and it never leaves it. Attach your own dumped BIOS with the BIOS picker for maximum accuracy.

7. Homebrew starter pack

The PS1 homebrew scene is small but genuinely active, a great low-stakes way to test the core. Every link verified live on 2026-10-11.

  • pdroms.de: PlayStation sectionPublic-domain PS1 titles from the classic archive.
  • itch.io, games tagged PlayStationIndie devs shipping playable PS1 homebrew; many free.
  • PSXDEVThe PS1 homebrew development community, tools and releases.
  • psx.devPS1 homebrew hub with modern tooling and demos.

Download on your own computer, then drag the file into the Player. The file is read locally, nothing uploads.

8. Prefer a desktop app? DuckStation

DuckStation (duckstation.org) is the modern PlayStation accuracy pick, gorgeous upscaling, per-game settings, and cheat support. It needs your own dumped BIOS for full accuracy (same rule as everywhere: dump your hardware, never download). The browser Player wins on zero-setup convenience.
