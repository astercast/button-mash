Rip-your-own-cartridges guide

Turn cartridges you own into verified ROM backups with a USB dumper — hardware picks, the dumping ritual, and the no-distribution rule.

  • ⏱ ~45 min
  • Beginner
Legal lane: this guide covers tools and techniques only. Supply ROMs you dumped from games you own, plus homebrew and public-domain titles. No ROM download links, no piracy sites — ever.

The idea

Dumping is reading the data off a cartridge or disc you own into a file on your computer. That file — the ROM or disc image — is a backup of your property, and it's the only legal way to get game files for the emulators and recompilers in the other guides.

This guide is practical how-to, not legal advice. The general principle is simple: back up what you own, and never distribute the copies.

The legal framing (general principles)

Making a personal backup of software you own is treated very differently from downloading or sharing copies you have no rights to. The bright line is distribution: uploading your dumps, sharing them with friends, or downloading someone else's is piracy regardless of whether you own the game.

Laws vary by country and change over time. When in doubt, the conservative read — your dumps, your devices, no sharing — keeps you safe everywhere.

Pick a dumper for your system

  • Game Boy / Game Boy Color / Game Boy Advance: GB Operator (Epilogue) or Joey Jr. — dead simple USB dumpers
  • SNES + Genesis: Retrode 2 — plug the cart into a USB adapter that mounts like a drive
  • Multi-system DIY: INLretro Dumper or the open-source Sanni Cart Reader — more systems, more tinkering
  • Save files: most of these dumpers also back up the save RAM, so your 100-hour file survives

The dumping ritual

1. Clean the cartridge's edge connector with isopropyl alcohol and a cotton swab — dirty contacts cause bad dumps
2. Seat the cart firmly in the dumper
3. Run the dumper software and save the ROM file
4. Verify it: good dumper software shows a checksum. Compare it against a known-good database entry for your exact cartridge — a match means a perfect read
5. Keep the physical cartridge. It's your proof of ownership and the reason the backup is yours to make

Disc-based games

Optical media needs a compatible disc drive plus dumping software; the per-system guides in preservation communities document exact drive models and settings. The same rule applies: your disc, your dump, no sharing. Disc dumps are big — budget storage accordingly.

Store it right

  • Keep each ROM next to a small text file with its checksum and the cartridge it came from
  • Back up save files separately; they're tiny and irreplaceable
  • Store everything twice (local drive + a backup) — dumped once, kept forever
  • Label homebrew and public-domain ROMs as such so your future self knows what's shareable
