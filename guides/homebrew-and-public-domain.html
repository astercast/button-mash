Homebrew & public-domain sources

Where to find legal homebrew ROMs and public-domain games: archives, dev communities, itch.io, and open-source originals.

  • ⏱ ~10 min
  • Reference
Legal lane: this guide covers tools and techniques only. Supply ROMs you dumped from games you own, plus homebrew and public-domain titles. No ROM download links, no piracy sites — ever.

What counts as legal

Homebrew means new games made by hobbyists for old (and new) hardware, released free by their authors. Public domain means the copyright expired or was explicitly waived. Both are fully legal to download, play, and share — they're the guilt-free way to fill an emulator library or a recompiler project.

Where to find them

  • pdroms.de — a long-running archive of homebrew ROMs across dozens of systems. Everything there is distributed by its authors.
  • nesdev.com — the NES homebrew development community: forums, tutorials, and released homebrew games.
  • gbdev.io — the Game Boy homebrew hub: dev resources plus finished, free games.
  • itch.io (itch.io/games, search 'homebrew') — many homebrew devs release ROMs free on their itch pages; filter by 'play in browser' for instant play.
  • Internet Archive collections — the MS-DOS Games, Internet Arcade, and Console Living Room collections (all in our game directory) include homebrew and public-domain titles; check each item's rights statement.

Open-source originals (not ROMs at all)

  • OpenTTD (openttd.org) — a complete, open-source transport sim and a genuinely great game
  • The open-source web games in our directory — 2048, Hextris, A Dark Room, and friends are free software you can also self-host
  • Engine recreations exist for some classics, but they typically need original game data from a copy you own — the engine is free, the assets aren't

The smell test

If a 'homebrew' site also offers commercial ROM downloads, leave. The test is simple: does the site point you at the game's author, or at a download button for a game Nintendo still sells? Link the guide and the tool, never the pirated content. When in doubt, the author's own page wins.
