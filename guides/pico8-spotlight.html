PICO-8 spotlight

The fantasy console's best corners: Splore, the Lexaloffle BBS, where the gems hide, how to play everything free in your browser — and how to make your first cart.

  • Needs: just a browser
  • ⏱ ~15 min
  • Beginner

01What PICO-8 is

PICO-8 is a "fantasy console" by Lexaloffle: a fake 1980s machine with deliberately tiny limits (128×128 pixels, 16 colors, 32KB cartridges) that runs on your real computer. The limits are the point — they force small, finished, weird games, and the community has made thousands of them.

The software itself costs $15 and is worth it if you want to make games. Playing other people's games in the browser is completely free.

02Splore: the built-in game browser

Inside PICO-8, type splore at the command prompt and hit enter. Splore is a graphical cartridge explorer: it browses games published to the community BBS over the internet, right from inside the console. Pick a cart, it downloads and runs. Press Esc to pause any cart and hop back to Splore.

It's the closest thing retro gaming has to a streaming service — except everything is tiny and free.

03The BBS: where the carts live

The Lexaloffle BBS is the official community hub: every published cartridge gets a page where anyone can play it in the browser — no PICO-8 purchase needed — star it, favorite it, and download the .p8.png cartridge file. The BBS sorts by featured, new, and top-rated, and the comments are where developers explain their tricks.

If Splore is the storefront, the BBS is the town square.

04Where the gems hide

Start with the BBS featured and top-rated sorts — curation by thousands of players beats any algorithm. Then follow developers: PICO-8's best creators publish repeatedly, and their BBS profiles are discographies.

Check the carts linked from this site's pico8-gems collection for a hand-picked starting shelf. And don't sleep on tweetcarts: games that fit in 140 characters, some of them genuinely brilliant.

05Playing in your browser (free)

Every BBS cartridge page has a built-in web player — click and play, no install, no account. Developers can also export any cart as a standalone HTML5 page, which is why PICO-8 games show up embedded across the web. Keyboard controls are the default (arrows + Z/X); many web exports also accept gamepads. Your progress saves in the browser like any web game.

06The pico8-gems shelf

This site's pico8-gems collection is the curated on-ramp: a Doom demake, the original Celeste, and other standout carts, each linking to its official BBS page. Play the shelf first, then use the BBS sorts above to go deeper.

If a shelf game hooks you, its BBS page links the developer's other carts — that's the rabbit hole, and it's a good one.

07Make your first cart (the 10-minute version)

If the $15 calls to you: PICO-8's editor is code, sprites, map, sound, and music in one window, all in Lua. The built-in help and the official manual walk you from a blank cart to a moving sprite in minutes. Publish to the BBS when it does something fun — the community plays and comments on everything, including gloriously broken first attempts.

The legal lane is clean by design: every cart is its author's original work.

08Why it matters

PICO-8 proved that constraints make better games and friendlier developers. Thousands of people finished and shipped something — most for the first time in their lives — because the canvas was small enough to fill. Whether you play the shelf, browse the BBS, or buy the console and make your own, you're participating in the healthiest corner of game development.

The short version: start with someone else's cart. End with your own.
