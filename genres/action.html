Genre deep dive · 15 games

Action games: move first, think later.

Action is the cabinet's pulse — 41 of the catalog's 213 games. This dive curates fifteen of them: the arena shooters, the .io arenas, the one-button endless runners, the physics-chaos brawlers, and the Flash-era run-and-gun legends. Every game below is free in the browser.

» Start here: the arena shooters

🔫

Krunker

Why it fits: the low-poly browser FPS — classes, maps, and gunplay the record itself calls surprisingly crisp. Start here; the rest of the family is measured in its shadow.

Controls: WASD + mouse

🥚

Shell Shockers

Why it fits: a multiplayer FPS where everyone is an egg with a gun. The record's verdict stands on its own: absurd and excellent.

Controls: WASD + mouse

🦸

Venge.io

Why it fits: the hero-based objective FPS: pick a hero, hold the point. The point is the game — everything else is how you keep it.

Controls: WASD + mouse

» .io arenas: grow or get eaten

💥

Diep.io

Why it fits: tank survival with deep upgrade trees — deceptively strategic, says the record, and the trees are exactly where the strategy hides.

Controls: WASD + mouse

🚀

Starblast.io

Why it fits: mine asteroids, upgrade your spaceship, dominate the sector. Three verbs, one arena, no wasted motion.

Controls: WASD / arrows + mouse

🏟️

arras.io

Why it fits: the open-source Diep.io successor: build your tank, pick your upgrades, and dominate the arena. The family business, opened up.

Controls: WASD + mouse

» One button, endless road

🏃

Canabalt

Why it fits: the one-button endless runner that popularized the endless runner. One button, one instruction, no excuses: jump or die.

Controls: One button (space / click)

⚡

Slope

Why it fits: steer a ball down an endless neon slope at breakneck speed. One wrong twitch and the run is over — the record promises nothing gentler.

Controls: Arrow keys / A-D

🌀

Run 3

Why it fits: the endless tunnel-runner: sprint through collapsing space tunnels, in the series' official web version. The floor is leaving; keep up.

Controls: Arrow keys / WASD

» Physics chaos

🦵

QWOP

Why it fits: run 100 meters with four keys controlling individual leg muscles. A masterpiece of suffering — the record's words, and the first ten meters back them up.

Controls: Q W O P keys

🛴

Happy Wheels

Why it fits: physics ragdoll chaos on a segway. A browser legend, still going — every crash is the physics engine's opinion, delivered at speed.

Controls: Arrows + space

🤼

Bonk.io

Why it fits: physics-based multiplayer brawls. The whole playbook is right there in the record: shove your friends off the map.

Controls: Arrows / WASD

» Flash-era run-and-gun

👖

Fancy Pants Adventure: World 1

Why it fits: Brad Borne's 2006 stick-figure platformer — the smoothest Flash run-and-slide ever, says the record — preserved on the Archive via Ruffle.

Controls: Arrow keys

👽

Alien Hominid (Flash)

Why it fits: Tom Fulp and Dan Paladin's 2002 run-and-gun: the little yellow alien's Newgrounds debut, preserved via Ruffle.

Controls: Arrow keys + Z/X

👊

Dad 'n Me

Why it fits: Tom Fulp and Dan Paladin's 2005 beat-em-up: a dad, his kid, and a city full of punks — Flash brawling at its finest, via Ruffle.

Controls: Arrow keys + A/S

What makes a game action? Your hands decide before your head finishes voting. These fifteen each test a different reflex — the arena shot (Krunker, Shell Shockers, Venge.io), the growing tank (Diep.io, Starblast.io, arras.io), the single button pressed at exactly the right moment (Canabalt, Slope, Run 3), the body as a physics problem (QWOP, Happy Wheels, Bonk.io), and the Flash-era run-and-gun (Fancy Pants Adventure, Alien Hominid, Dad 'n Me). The other 26 action games in the catalog — more arena shooters and .io pits, the PICO-8 pair, racers and stunt tracks, and a factory defended by conveyor-fed production lines — are all on the directory shelf.
