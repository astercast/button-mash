The DOS corner. Your disks, in a tab.

DOS games never needed a console ROM; they need their files and a DOS to stand on. Drop a ZIP or a folder of a DOS game you own, and this page wraps it into a .jsdos bundle right here, DOSBox config included, then boots it. The bundle is yours: download it, keep it, re-play it here any time. Nothing uploads, ever.

💾

Drop your DOS game here

A ZIP of the game's files, the game's folder, or a ready-made .jsdos bundle. The start program is picked automatically (a start.bat wins; you can overrule it below).

Waiting for a game. DOSBox is the engine, provided by the open-source js-dos project; the engine loads from its CDN on first play, your game files never do.
DOSBOX, VIA JS-DOS

The screen lights up here when a bundle plays. Keyboard and mouse are the controls, exactly as the games expect; click the screen to give it focus.

🛡️ Legal lane: bring games you own: your old floppies and CDs, installed folders from your own machine, or the many DOS games their makers now give away. This corner wraps and plays your files; it provides none. Read the rules · How to get your disks onto this computer

» Bundles kept on this device

    Kept in this browser's storage, addressed by content, deleted whenever you say. Export the bundle file itself for a copy that outlives any browser.

    » How this corner works, honestly

    The bundle is the converter output

    For consoles, the Converter makes a web page. For DOS, the honest portable form is the .jsdos bundle: your game's files plus a small dosbox.conf that mounts the folder and runs the start program. It plays here and in any js-dos player, and it is built entirely on your machine.

    DOSBox now, DOSBox-X later

    The default backend is DOSBox, the fast, compatible choice for DOS games. The heavier DOSBox-X build is reserved for the Windows 3.x-on-DOS cases and is not the default, because most DOS games do not need it and all of them pay for it in speed.

    Windows games are a different door

    A game that needs real Windows (95, 98, XP and later) is not a DOS game, and this corner will not pretend a DOSBox wrap makes it one. Some have excellent source ports that read your original files; the guide shows how to find yours.
