For parents & teachers

The parent guide.

How Kids corner curation actually works, what we can and cannot promise, and practical tricks for calmer game time.

How the curation works

Every game in our directory went through a four-bar audit to earn a spot in Kids corner:

  1. No open chat with strangers. If players can talk to each other — text, voice, or lobby chat — the game is out.
  2. No unmoderated multiplayer chat or user-generated content. That includes player names everyone can see (like in Agar.io) and games whose whole point is chatting or drawing for strangers.
  3. No gambling mechanics. No loot boxes, no betting, no casino-style loops.
  4. No graphic or horror content. No gore, no jump scares, no horror genre — even the "mild" ones.
The honesty policy These flags are our best assessments, not guarantees. Websites change, and we re-audit on every wave — but a game could change between audits. Games marked 👀 Play together first are ones we could not fully verify end-to-end. The standing rule: play the first 10 minutes together with any new game. If something feels off, close the tab and tell us — a game that does not pass gets removed, never defended.

Games that do not pass are excluded entirely — we never soften a game's description to make it fit. 70 of our 114 games did not make the cut, including genuinely good games with lobby chat or content we could not verify. That is the point: a short list you can trust is better than a long list you have to police.

What "No chat" means (and what it doesn't)

Every game in Kids corner carries a 🔇 No chat badge. That means the game itself gives strangers no way to contact your child — no chat boxes, no voice, no friend requests. It does not mean:

  • The host website has no ads (a few games run on ad-heavy portals — the parent note on each card says so plainly).
  • Your child cannot share the link with a friend. They can — that is just sharing, not chatting.
  • The game is educational or age-perfect. Age bands (5–7, 8–10, 11–12) are difficulty and theme guidance, not developmental endorsements.

Screen-time tips (common sense, not a doctor's note)

We are a game directory, not a health authority — so here is only what works in real living rooms:

  • Decide the shape of the session before it starts: "two games, then off" beats "ten more minutes" every time. Puzzles with natural endings (daily word games, a Sudoku grid) make stopping easy; endless runners do not.
  • Keep the first session together. Ten minutes of co-play teaches you the game's loop — and teaches them that new games are something you check out with a grown-up.
  • End on a win, not a timer. If a game has levels, "finish this level, then we're done" causes less friction than a clock.
  • Mix the bands. A calm 5–7 sandbox (Townscaper, Sandspiel) after a hard 11–12 puzzle is a good wind-down order.
  • Watch for the ad-heavy hosts. A few games carry an ad warning in their parent note — those are the ones to supervise or open with an ad blocker.

The "one more level" negotiation trick

Our Player supports save states — freezing a game exactly where it is and thawing it later. For browser games in Kids corner, most remember their own progress too. The negotiation script that works:

  1. "Finish this level, then save." The save state (or the game's own save) turns "stop now" into "pause here" — the game will be exactly where they left it.
  2. Save together the first time. Let them watch the state get saved and reloaded — once they trust the pause, the meltdown loses its fuel.
  3. Name the return time. "We'll unpause it after lunch" is concrete; "later" is not.

This only applies to games with real progress — endless high-score chasers (Slope, QWOP) end on a number, so "beat your best, then done" is the cleaner deal there.

For teachers

Kids corner games run entirely in the browser — nothing to install, no accounts for the kid-safe picks (one idle game may prompt for an optional account; its parent note says so). Good classroom fits: Seterra and Globle for geography, Sudoku and 2048 for logic warm-ups, Song Maker for music, Quick, Draw! for a five-minute energizer. See skills/classroom-games.md for the full classroom playbook.

Found a problem? If a Kids corner game changed, shows unexpected content, or asks for something it should not — stop playing it and treat the tab as closed. Tell us what you saw (the game title and what happened), and we will re-audit and pull it if needed. Exclusion is always the default.
