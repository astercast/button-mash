# TAXONOMY BRIEF — the cozy/horror shelf call

**For: a human ruling. Status: UNDECIDED — nothing in this document
moves a game. Prepared wave 27 (2026-10-12), from evidence gathered
in waves 25–27. The two games in question stay pinned in `horror`
(`test_curator25`/`26`/`27` enforce it) until this call is made.**

## The question

Two games — **purrgatory** and **Lookouts** — sit on the horror
shelf. Both were shelved on setting (an afterlife; a desert with
rumours) rather than on scares. Wave 24 set the house rule this
catalog now shelves by: *genre follows the game's own description,
not its furniture.* By that rule both entries look mis-shelved. The
Curator's recommendation is at the bottom; the evidence comes first,
and the call is yours.

## The census effect, exactly

- Today: **213 games — horror 6, cozy 16.**
- If both games move: **horror 6 → 4, cozy 16 → 18.** Nothing else
  changes; no game enters or leaves the catalog.
- The four horror games that would remain: *We Become What We
  Behold*, *Adventures With Anxiety*, *Dating Killmulator 2*, and
  *Little Troubles in Spooky Town* (its own case is below — if it
  also moves, horror is 3 and cozy is 19).

## Evidence — purrgatory (currently horror)

- **Its own store page** (itch.io, checked 2026-10-12): "a visual
  novel about passing time in an afterlife where nothing really
  matters. explore a whimsical point-and-click world, befriend seven
  charming characters, and remember — furever's not so bad!" The page
  carries no horror framing, no horror tag, and no content warnings;
  it leads with a game-jam win and a Museum of Pop Culture feature.
- **This catalog's own description** already agrees: "A hand-drawn
  point-and-click set in cat-filled purgatory. Melancholy and sweet."
  Nothing in the entry claims a scare.
- The only horror signal is the furniture: purgatory, ghosts, cats
  who are dead. By the wave-24 rule, furniture is not genre.

## Evidence — Lookouts (currently horror)

- **Its own store page** (itch.io, checked 2026-10-12): "Lookouts is
  a romance visual novel about two gay trans masc outlaws in the old
  west, finding refuge in each other, and a little hope for a better
  life." ~45,000 words, a 5–6 hour read, expanded from a Gay Western
  Jam entry. The word it leads with is *romance*.
- **Its content warnings** are western-drama material: alcohol,
  smoking, gun violence, gun death, blood, injury, mild transphobia,
  discussions of racism and settler violence — with the page's own
  note that injuries "aren't described in graphic detail." Nothing
  supernatural, nothing staged to frighten.
- **This catalog's own description** already hedges: "A story-rich
  western visual novel about two outlaws in the desert.
  (Spooky-tinged.)" — the parenthesis is doing all the horror work,
  and "tinged" is not a genre.

## The comparable shelving (same catalog, already ruled)

- **Six Cats Under** — a ghost rescuing cats in a point-and-click
  afterlife — sits in **cozy**. It is purrgatory's nearest neighbour
  in the whole catalog: same form, same afterlife, same cats.
- **goodbye, doggy** — help a family as their ghostly dog; "short,
  tender" — sits in **cozy**. A ghost protagonist has never, by
  itself, put a game on the horror shelf here.
- **Fallen London** — a gothic narrative RPG under a stolen London,
  all gaslight and dread atmosphere — sits in **cozy**, because the
  experience it sells is story, not fear.
- **The horror shelf's centre** is dread you feel in your body:
  *We Become What We Behold* (a five-minute game about hate that
  "will sit with you"), *Adventures With Anxiety* (you play as the
  anxiety), *Dating Killmulator 2* (every date might be a killer).
  Neither flagged game aims at that feeling, and neither's store
  page claims to.

## The related case — Little Troubles in Spooky Town

The sixth horror entry, and the natural third name in this ruling.
Its own itch page (checked 2026-10-12) tags it **Cozy, Cute, Ghosts,
whimsical, Wholesome**, lists its genre as Adventure, and says it
was made in nine days for the Girly Game Jam; its stated goal is to
"prove to everyone that you can be whatever you want to be." This
catalog's entry already reads "Spooky-cozy, not spooky-scary." If
the ruling moves the two flagged games on own-description evidence,
the same evidence moves this one too — the brief flags it so the
human can rule on all three in one pass, whichever way it goes.

## The case for leaving them (stated fairly)

- Shelves are also browsing aids: a visitor hunting "spooky-season"
  games in October may be glad to find a ghost story under horror
  even if it is gentle. Moving both leaves horror at four (or
  three), a thin shelf.
- Lookouts does contain gun death and blood; a reader who filters
  by shelf to avoid dark material entirely is arguably served by
  the current placement, even if "horror" is the wrong word for it.
- Neither counter-argument survives contact with the cozy shelf's
  existing contents (Fallen London is darker than both), but they
  are the honest reasons a human might rule the other way.

## Recommendation

**Move purrgatory and Lookouts to cozy, and let the same ruling
settle Little Troubles in Spooky Town.** The catalog's own rule —
genre follows the game's own description — plus each game's own
store page points one way, and the two closest comparable entries
(Six Cats Under, goodbye, doggy) already sit in cozy on identical
evidence. Horror keeps the games that try to scare or unsettle,
which is what the shelf is for.
