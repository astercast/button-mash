Genre deep dive · 15 games

Puzzle games: think first, click second.

Puzzle is the biggest shelf in the cabinet: 48 of the catalog's 213 games. This dive curates fifteen of them: the daily rituals, the falling-block esports, the word games that fight back, and the logic grids that never run out. Every game below is free in the browser.

» Start here

🔢

2048

Why it fits: the genre's front door. Slide numbered tiles, merge matching pairs, chase the 2048 tile. One rule, a board that punishes greed, and a restart that is always one keystroke away, deceptively simple, endlessly restartable.

Controls: Arrow keys / swipe

🟩

Wordle

Why it fits: one five-letter word a day, six guesses, and the whole internet solving the same puzzle. The green-and-yellow grid turned deduction into a daily ritual, and spawned half the word shelf below.

Controls: Keyboard

🧩

Simon Tatham's Puzzles

Why it fits: 40+ hand-crafted logic puzzles: Loopy, Mines, Net, Map and dozens more, as official JavaScript builds. No timers, no lives, just a fresh grid and your own reasoning. The puzzle collection other collections wish they were.

Controls: Mouse

» Falling blocks, played seriously

🧱

TETR.IO

Why it fits: blisteringly fast modern Tetris with ranked matchmaking, the esport of falling blocks. The puzzle is the same seven pieces it always was; the opponent across the screen is what changed.

Controls: Keyboard (customizable)

⚡

Jstris

Why it fits: no-frills multiplayer Tetris in the browser, pure block-stacking competition with the decoration stripped off. When you want the falling-block puzzle and nothing else.

Controls: Keyboard (customizable)

🏆

Tetris (Official)

Why it fits: the official free Tetris in your browser, with Marathon, sprint and daily challenges on the real deal. The benchmark every other stacker on this shelf is quietly measured against.

Controls: Keyboard (customizable)

» Word games that fight back

🟨

Connections

Why it fits: sort sixteen words into four secret groups. Every board hides decoys that belong to two groups at once, which is why it is much harder than it looks, the wrong group always feels right first.

Controls: Mouse / touch

😈

Absurdle

Why it fits: Wordle's adversarial twin, the game changes its answer to dodge your guesses for as long as it can. You are not solving a word; you are cornering one. Bring your best opener.

Controls: Keyboard, type guesses

🌡️

Semantle

Why it fits: guess the secret word by meaning, every guess is scored on semantic similarity, not spelling. A puzzle about how words sit next to each other in your head; strangely philosophical by guess forty.

Controls: Keyboard, type guesses

🔑

The Password Game

Why it fits: a password field with increasingly unhinged rules, each new rule breaking the password the last one forced you to build. A constraint puzzle wearing a login form as a disguise. You will lose. You will laugh.

Controls: Keyboard

» Combine and create

⚗️

Little Alchemy 2

Why it fits: combine earth, fire, air and water to discover 700+ items. Every pairing is a tiny hypothesis, what do you get when you mix mud and fire?, and the answer is always one drag away. Digital tinkering at its finest.

Controls: Mouse / touch

♾️

Infinite Craft

Why it fits: infinite combinations from four starting elements, with no fixed recipe book to finish. Where Little Alchemy 2 is a list to complete, this is a space to wander, craft 'philosophy' before lunch.

Controls: Mouse

» Pure logic grids

✏️

Sudoku

Why it fits: fast, clean browser Sudoku with note mode. Zero friction, all logic, the grid, the pencil marks, and the moment a whole row falls into place at once.

Controls: Mouse / keyboard

🔴

0hh1

Why it fits: a tiny perfect logic puzzle, fill the grid red or blue with no three in a row. One rule stated in a breath, fresh puzzles every day, and not one wasted click in the design.

Controls: Mouse / touch

⭐

Dare To Doku

Why it fits: a 'star battle' logic puzzle, deduce where every player hides in grids of 4-10 across 10,000 unique levels. A GBA Jam 2026 homebrew that plays in the browser, and proof the grid puzzle still has new shapes in it.

Controls: Mouse

What makes a puzzle game? A small set of rules, a state you can read at a glance, and a solution that feels obvious the second after you find it. These fifteen each lean on a different muscle, spatial (2048, the falling blocks), verbal (Wordle, Connections, Semantle), adversarial (Absurdle, The Password Game), combinatorial (the alchemy pair), and pure deduction (Sudoku, 0hh1, Dare To Doku). The other 33 puzzle games in the catalog, geography quizzes, interactive essays, coding challenges and more, are all on the directory shelf.
